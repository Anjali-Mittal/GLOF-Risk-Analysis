Verified Positive Detection

South Lhonak Lake (Tista Basin, Sikkim)

Catastrophic outburst on 4 October 2023. Real post-event satellite drainage confirmed.

Actual Drain Event: Lake surface dropped from 1.130 km² (2022) to 0.975 km² (2023 Sentinel-2), confirming catastrophic moraine dam breach after an ice/rock avalanche trigger (Mohanty et al., 2026).
Pipeline Risk Probability: 0.997 (Rank #2 Regional Threat)
Stored Volume: 40.9 – 44.6 MCM (Cook-Quincey: 44.6, Evans: 42.0, Huggel: 40.9)
Peak Discharge (Qp): 8,000 – 33,404 m³/s (Primary Median: 25,291 m³/s; Froehlich: 9,322 m³/s)
Moraine Ring Slope: 17.5° (Steep fraction = 16.4%)
5-Day Monsoon Extreme: 135.6 mm
Known Issue: Polygon Mismatch

Thame Glacial Lake (Koshi Basin, Nepal)

Outburst event on 16 August 2024. Audit of inventory polygon vs real burst source.

Polygon Mismatch Audit: The actual lake that burst in August 2024 was a small supraglacial pond (~0.05 km²) releasing ~0.3–0.38 MCM. Automated polygon I2015_GL086586E27928N tracks an adjacent 0.55 km² proglacial body (~15 MCM) which did not drain (area was 0.54 km² in 2024 vs 0.55 km² in 2022).
Pipeline Risk Probability: 0.675 (Classified as Moderate Risk)
Pipeline Volume (Tracked Lake): 15.1 MCM (~40× larger than actual ~0.35 MCM breach)
Pipeline Peak Discharge (Qp): 4,600 – 13,889 m³/s (Reflects adjacent large body)
Validation Finding: The claim of "2/2 detection" is invalid for Thame. Standard 30m inventory polygons do not resolve micro-supraglacial ponds.
